# EyeCare 0.7 research engine

The Android app uses user-selected nominal domain weights **screen 26%, environment 22%, posture 8%, usage 18%, eyes 26%**. These weights were not learned from user records or clinically validated. Each measured feature becomes a bounded 0–100 relative burden proxy. Available features are averaged within a domain with their quality; available domains are then averaged using `nominal weight × domain quality`. Missing values never become zero. Dark-environment and near-distance burden are each counted once inside their domains. The 0.7 scenario addition cap is **0 points** to avoid overlapping bonuses.

The light sensor reports ambient **lux**, while the OS brightness control is a relative setting rather than emitted luminance. `E08.darkBrightRisk` is a bounded heuristic that increases when measured lux falls and a higher brightness setting is observed. Its 50 lux transition is an engineering hypothesis, not a medical or exposure threshold. If either source is missing, the feature is missing. The app does not infer retinal dose or permanent damage.

The front camera estimates **camera-axis** eye distance from iris ring diameter and camera geometry: `distance_cm ≈ focal_pixels × 11.7 mm / iris_pixels / 10`. The uncalibrated 11.7 mm iris diameter is a population assumption. If the user measures a 30 cm eye-to-camera reference with a ruler, the saved scale is `30 cm × reference_iris_pixels` and future distance is `scale / iris_pixels`. Calibration is tied to camera ID, frame dimensions, crop geometry, and rotation. Missing camera geometry or unstable/occluded iris yields no centimetre estimate unless there is matching user calibration. Low exposure and inconsistent eyes also yield missing values. The 15–80 cm operating range and quality gates are engineering limits, not measured accuracy guarantees. MediaPipe normalized landmark `z` is never treated as centimetres.

For a report, valid neighbouring frame intervals up to two seconds contribute time. The typical distance is their time-weighted median. The app also reports valid observation coverage and the time share under 30 cm; missing intervals are not classified as safe. `D02.nearExposureRisk` maps shorter valid distance to a larger bounded relative burden within the usage domain. Camera frames, landmarks, intrinsics, and calibration scale remain local. From app 0.7.2, consented new observations upload only six aggregate distance fields: estimated centimetres, composite estimate quality, time coverage, calibration status, valid minutes, and window minutes. `distance_estimate_quality` is **already frame quality multiplied by time coverage** and must not be multiplied by coverage again. Missing distance stays null, while a zero-valid-minute window can be recorded. The old `calibrated_distance_cm` field keeps its original meaning and is not filled from the new estimate. Existing owner-only sync and immutable observations remain unchanged; already inserted rows cannot be backfilled through UPDATE.

Viewing distance cannot yield a visual acuity (for example 0.x) or glasses prescription. A proper visual acuity test requires recognition of standardized optotypes. The camera measures toward its lens, which differs from distance to the display centre. The public editor can reconstruct stored domain aggregates by score version, but it cannot exactly reproduce the phone score or infer distance in historical rows that predate the six-field contract.

Evidence and limitations:

- [Google MediaPipe Iris research](https://research.google/blog/mediapipe-iris-real-time-iris-tracking-depth-estimation/) describes iris-depth geometry and the population iris-size assumption. Its reported experimental error is not an accuracy claim for this app.
- [Android Camera2 intrinsics](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/hardware/camera2/CameraCharacteristics#LENS_INTRINSIC_CALIBRATION) describes optional camera calibration values; this app scales them for the selected stream or uses device physical/focal metadata when available.
- [Android ambient light sensor](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/sensors/sensors_environment) describes device lux measurement.
- [Smartphone distance and eyestrain study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27716998/) supports investigating near work but does not validate this score or infer acuity.
- [Visual acuity testing](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003396.htm) requires a standardized vision test.

Validation still needed on real devices: ruler references at 20/25/30/40/50/60 cm; different front cameras, resolutions, rotations and zoom; glasses, lighting, occlusion and head pose; calibrated versus uncalibrated estimates; camera CPU, battery use and heat. Unit tests verify geometry and missingness logic only.
